03 — Library

Worth your hours, and the ones that aren't.

Books and podcasts we've actually finished, each with a note on which parts hold up and which are the author telling a good story. Recommending something doesn't mean swallowing all of it.

Suggest one Read the hacks instead

No affiliate links

Nothing on this page pays us anything. There are no referral codes, no sponsored slots and no publisher deals — if a book is here, it's because someone read it and argued about it in the Discord.

Books

Start with these four.

All Longevity Training Sleep Nutrition Thinking about evidence

Outlive — Peter Attia

The best single explanation of why prevention beats treatment, and of the four diseases that will most likely be the ones that get you. The framework is excellent; the specific protocols run well ahead of the evidence behind them.

worth the time

Read it for the framing of risk over decades. Discount the supplement sections.

Endure — Alex Hutchinson

What actually limits endurance, and how much of it is in the head. Hutchinson is a journalist who reads the papers properly and tells you when a finding is shaky — which is roughly the house style we're aiming for.

worth the time

Why We Sleep — Matthew Walker

The book that got most of us caring about sleep, and a genuinely gripping read. It has also drawn detailed, well-sourced criticism for overstating several specific claims. Worth reading with that in mind rather than as a reference.

great read, contested numbers

Read alongside the published critiques — there's a thread in #sleep with the links.

Bad Science — Ben Goldacre

Not a health book, a bullshit-detection book. Fifteen years old and still the fastest way to learn why a study can be technically true and completely useless. If you only read one thing here, read this one first.

worth the time

Podcasts

For the commute, with caveats.

Podcasts are where good research and confident nonsense sound identical. These are the ones where the ratio is best — but even the good ones have episodes we'd skip.

Sigma Nutrition Radio

Dense, slow, and allergic to hype. Danny Lennon walks through what a body of evidence actually shows, including when the answer is boring. The least entertaining podcast on this list and probably the most useful.

dense, no hype

The Drive — Peter Attia

Three-hour interviews that go far deeper into method than almost anything else in the genre. Long, sometimes repetitive, and the supplement conversations deserve the same scepticism as the book.

deep, very long

Huberman Lab

Superb at explaining mechanisms and how the body works. Weaker when it jumps from mechanism to protocol — the leap from "this pathway exists" to "do this for six weeks" often isn't supported by trials. Great teaching, take the recommendations lightly.

great teaching, loose protocols

Found My Fitness — Rhonda Patrick

Deep on molecular biology and the source of a lot of the sauna and heat interest. Strong on mechanism, and prone to reading a lot into observational data — the same caution applies as with our own sauna note.

strong mechanism, thin protocols
