05 — Adventures

Where the theory meets a bad day.

A protocol that works in a spreadsheet is not the same as a protocol that works at 3,000 metres with cold hands and no appetite. These are the long days out — what we were testing, what held up, and the part where it fell apart.

Why this counts, and why it doesn't

One person on one mountain proves nothing. What a day out is good for is finding the practical failures a trial never sees: the gel you can't stomach, the plan that assumed you'd sleep.

Aneto, 3,404 m

The highest point in the Pyrenees, in a single long day. The plan was to hold 70 g of carbs an hour above 3,000 m and see whether the appetite drop everyone warns about was manageable with liquid calories.

July 2026 write-up

What we got wrong: everything solid became inedible above the hut. The plan survived only because half the calories were drinkable. Fed straight back into the carbs at altitude note.

First 100 km

A flat, hot road hundred with no crew. The test was whether a Zone 2 base built over the winter actually holds up past hour eight, when everything you've read stops being relevant and pacing becomes a decision you make every ten minutes.

May 2026 write-up

What we got wrong: the heat. Twelve weeks of aerobic work and no heat adaptation at all — which is exactly why the heat block below exists.

Six weeks of heat before a hot race

Deliberate heat acclimation squeezed into a normal working week: sauna after sessions, no fan on the turbo, and an honest count of how many of the planned exposures actually happened.

in progress week 4 of 6

Running now. Notes going up in #training as it happens — 41 people in the community are running a version of this too.

A week sleeping at altitude

Seven nights above 2,000 m, tracking sleep and resting heart rate on the way up and back down. Mostly to find out how badly the wearable numbers get distorted before drawing any conclusions from them.

How a write-up works

The failures get the most space.

01

Say what's being tested

Before setting off, in writing: the hack, the plan, and what would count as it not working.

02

Log it as it happens

Watch data, food, sleep, weather. Memory rewrites a hard day within about a week.

03

Publish the mess

Including the part where the plan was abandoned at hour six. That's usually the only genuinely useful bit.
