06 — Wiki

Who takes what, and whether it holds up.

A record of the protocols that public figures in longevity actually follow — with the receipt for every line, what each of them sells, what they quietly stopped taking, who publicly refuses it, and the evidence sitting next to the claim rather than three clicks away. Popularity is not evidence, so this page never lets the two share a column.

How to read this page

Four rules, and they are the whole point of it existing.

  • Provenance has three tiers — blue is linked and checkable, amber is pending, red has no located source at all.
  • The tiers never get added together, because "we found it but couldn't read it" is not the same claim as "here it is".
  • Who sells what is stated first, before their protocol, not in a footnote.
  • Refusals count too — someone declining a compound is data the headcount hides.

    The protocols

    One person, one open file.

    Commercial interests come first on every card, deliberately. Not because someone selling a supplement is lying, but because you cannot weigh a recommendation without knowing who profits from it. Where a person sells or advises on something that also appears in their own stack, the card says so out loud. The badge on each card counts how much of that protocol we have read at its source rather than taken on trust.

    The graveyard

    What they took, and quietly stopped.

    Every stack you read online is the survivor. The abandoned compounds get deleted from the page and the recommendation carries on as if nothing happened. Here they stay, with the date and the stated reason — it's the most informative section on the site and the rarest anywhere else.

    Method

    Five questions, not one score.

    A single number out of ten is the most seductive and least honest thing a page like this can do — it hides which part is weak. Every substance here is answered along five separate axes, and any of them is allowed to say "nobody has checked".

    01

    Evidence

    How much has actually been tested, and did the trials agree with each other. Trials in humans, not mechanism in a dish.

    02

    Effect size

    In plain words. "Statistically significant" and "worth doing" are different sentences, and the gap between them is where most of this industry lives.

    03

    In whom

    Studied in whom, exactly. Deficient people, sick people, 22-year-old men, mice. A result rarely travels well outside the room it was measured in.

    04

    Risk

    What it costs when it goes wrong, and what happens over years rather than weeks — the part almost never measured in the trials people quote.

    05

    Freshness

    When we last read the literature on it. A page that never says this is asking you to trust an unknown quantity of drift.

    Provenance

    Three tiers, kept apart on purpose.

    source · example.com

    Primary. The person said it themselves, or a company announced it: their own site, their own post, a disclosure page, a press release. This is the only tier we treat as established.

    pending · stated on air

    Pending. We have found where the original lives and it is not a page anyone can link a line to — it was said on a podcast, or the detailed version sits behind a membership on their own site. The figure is shown because hiding it helps nobody, but it is counted on its own line and never presented as established. Hover any pending pill and it tells you which of the two it is.

    unverified

    Nothing. Widely repeated, no receipt located. It stays on the page, greyed out and labelled, until someone finds one or proves it wrong.

    What this page refuses to do

    The four ways a wiki like this normally goes bad.

    • Turn a headcount into evidence. Adoption and proof get separate columns and are never averaged into one number.
    • Launder provenance. A figure we have not read at its source is never displayed as though we had.
    • Hide the seller. If someone sells, advises, or holds equity in what they recommend, it appears above their protocol — flagged when it overlaps their own stack.
    • Quietly delete the failures. Dropped compounds move to the graveyard with a date. Nothing disappears.

    Nothing on this page is a recommendation, and none of it is medical advice. It is a record of what other people say they do — which is a different thing from what you should.

    Every amber pill on this page is a job.

    Amber means the answer is in an episode nobody has timestamped yet, or behind a membership one of us doesn't have. Red means we haven't found it at all. Both are to-do items, not decoration — and the amber ones are small: one episode, one minute mark, one dose. If you listen to these people already, you can close one tonight. Bring it to the Discord and it goes up with your name on it.
