Protocol 0

The human baseline.

Before supplements. Before blood panels. Before wearables. Before cold plunges. Before trying to slow aging. Get your baseline right.

The minimum health protocol for almost every adult. It costs close to nothing and needs no exotic technology. If you aren't doing these six things consistently, most advanced biohacking is optimising the wrong problem.

01Sleep7–9 hours a night 02Train150–300 min cardio + 2× strength weekly 03EatMostly minimally processed. Plants. Enough protein. 04AvoidNo tobacco. Alcohol low. 05MeasureBlood pressure and basic prevention 06OralBrush twice. Clean between teeth daily.

Six, not a hundred. Tick each one off as it becomes true for you — the counter at the top of the page follows along.

01

Sleep

Target

7–9 hours a night

Actual sleep. Not hours in bed with a phone.

The protocol

  1. Give yourself 7–9 hours to sleepNot seven hours in bed while spending one of them on your phone. The window has to be bigger than the target.
  2. Keep the schedule consistentWake and sleep at roughly the same time, including at weekends.
  3. Make the bedroom boringDark, quiet, comfortable, cool enough to sleep well.
  4. Protect the final hourLess work, less stimulation, less of whatever predictably keeps you up.
  5. Fix persistent bad sleepIf you give yourself the time and still wake exhausted, don't file that under normal. That's a conversation with a doctor.

When sleep goes, everything downstream gets harder: training, food choices, energy, recovery, attention, mood. It's first on the list because it makes the other five cheaper.

Measure

  • Sleep duration
  • Bedtime consistency
  • How rested you feel

No wearable needed. If you already own one, use it for trends across weeks — not for grading last night.

02

Train

Cardio

150–300 min a week

Moderate intensity

or

75–150 min a week

Vigorous intensity — or an equivalent mix of the two

Strength

2+ days a week

All the major muscle groups

The protocol

  1. Strength, two or three timesSquat, hinge, push, pull, carry. That covers it.
  2. Cardio, two to four timesRun, cycle, swim, row, walk fast, hike, play a sport. The machine doesn't matter.
  3. Move through the dayStairs, walking, getting up from the desk. Some movement is substantially better than none.

Your first objective isn't Zone 2, or lactate, or a VO₂ max protocol. It's building the habit of training. The best programme is the one you can sustain for years without repeatedly getting injured or quitting.

Measure

  • Strength sessions a week
  • Cardio minutes a week

That's enough to start. VO₂ max, resting heart rate, strength standards, body composition, HRV, lactate — all interesting, none of them Protocol 0.

03

Eat

Every day

400 g fruit & vegetables

and

25 g fibre

Naturally occurring, for anyone over ten

Most of what you eat should look like food.

Not a product engineered to be impossible to stop eating. You don't need to eliminate every processed food, fear carbohydrates, fear fat, or eat exactly like Bryan Johnson or Peter Attia.

Default to

  1. Plants, in quantityVegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, nuts, seeds.
  2. Enough proteinQuality sources, spread across the day rather than banked into one meal.
  3. Mostly unsaturated fatsAnd enough energy overall for what you're asking your body to do.
  4. Food you'll actually keep eatingA diet you abandon in March scores zero, whatever its theoretical merits.

Get 80–90% of your diet good first. Then argue about the last 10% — blueberries versus raspberries, olive oil brand A versus B, twelve-hour fasts versus fourteen. Not yet.

Keep low

  • Free sugar
  • Excess sodium
  • Trans fats
  • Big calories, little nutrition

WHO puts free sugars below 10% of energy intake, with further benefit below 5%.

04

Avoid

Health isn't only about adding things. Often the highest-return move is removing one.

Tobacco

Zero

All forms are harmful, and there's no safe level of exposure

Alcohol

Less

The lower the exposure, the lower the risk. There's no completely risk-free level.

What this does and doesn't mean

  1. If you smoke, quitting outranks everythingIt deserves priority over any supplement, device or longevity intervention on this site.
  2. It doesn't mean one beer will kill youIt means don't file alcohol under longevity supplement. If you enjoy it, treat it as a trade-off you're choosing.

Where to get help

Quitting is the one item here where doing it alone is measurably harder than doing it with support. Your GP is the fastest route to what actually works.

05

Measure

Bryan Johnson measures almost everything. You don't need to. Start where an abnormal result would actually change what you do.

Start here

Know your blood pressure

It can run high for years without a single symptom. Screening for it carries the USPSTF's highest recommendation grade, with elevated readings confirmed outside the clinic before any treatment.

Then keep prevention current

  • Blood lipids
  • Blood glucose
  • Vaccinations
  • Cancer screening
  • Sexual-health screening
  • Dental care
  • Anything your family history points at

Which of these apply, and how often, depends on your age and risk. That's a conversation with a clinician, not a checklist from a website.

The measurement rule

Don't measure something just because you can. Measure it because the measurement is meaningful, you understand what it represents, and the result could change an action.

A dashboard with 150 biomarkers isn't automatically better than knowing the five numbers that matter for you.

06

Oral

The least exciting section on hackers.bio. Do it anyway.

Every day

Brush 2×

With fluoride toothpaste

and

Clean between 1×

Floss or another interdental method that suits your mouth

Two minutes a day, roughly the cost of nothing, and it's the fundamental most often skipped by people who own a cold plunge.

That's it. The whole protocol.

Not because everything below is useless. Because none of it deserves to come before the baseline.

Your day

Morning

  • Wake around your normal time
  • Move
  • Eat to the baseline
  • Brush your teeth
  • Take what you specifically need — not somebody else's stack

Day

  • Move often
  • Train if it's scheduled
  • Mostly minimally processed food
  • Plants and protein in the meals
  • No tobacco
  • Alcohol rarely, or deliberately

Night

  • Finish the day so you can sleep
  • Brush
  • Clean between your teeth
  • Leave yourself 7–9 hours
  • Repeat

Never use an advanced protocol to compensate for a broken basic one.

Creatine doesn't fix inactivity. A CGM doesn't fix your diet. Red light doesn't fix chronic sleep deprivation. Sauna doesn't cancel smoking. A longevity blood panel doesn't make you healthy.

Master the baseline. Then hack your biology.

The hacks assume Protocol 0 is already running. If it isn't, most of them are optimising the wrong problem.
