About

A notebook, kept in public.

hackers.bio started as a folder of notes about what actually works on a human body. Putting it online was mostly a way to be forced to get it right.

The short version

  • What — notes on biohacks, graded by how much anyone really knows.
  • Who — one person writing, and a community that corrects him.
  • Money — nothing for sale, no affiliate links, no sponsors.

Most health content online has the same problem: it sounds equally confident whether it's describing forty years of trials or a mechanism someone found interesting on a podcast. You can't tell the difference from the outside, and the confident version usually travels further.

This site tries the opposite. Every hack says how sure anyone actually is, in plain words. Some notes end with "nobody has checked this properly", and those stay published too.

Who writes it

One person, mostly — someone who reads too many papers, trains a lot, and gets things wrong in public. The corrections come from the community, and ninety-six of them have changed a note so far. That ratio is the point: this works better as an argument than as a broadcast.

What it isn't

It isn't medical advice, and it isn't a substitute for a doctor who knows your history. Nothing here can account for your medication, your conditions or your blood work. If a note contradicts your clinician, believe your clinician.

It also isn't a shop. There's no supplement line, no coaching upsell, no affiliate links in the library. If that ever changes it will be stated at the top of the page, not buried in a disclosure.

How it's built

Static pages, plain markdown notes, deployed from a git repository. The whole thing is deliberately cheap to run so it never needs to earn its keep by selling you something.

Get in touch

The fastest route is the Discord — corrections, questions and arguments all land better there than in an inbox. For anything else, hello@hackers.bio.
